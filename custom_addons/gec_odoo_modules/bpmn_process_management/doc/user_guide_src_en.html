1.What the module does

The module is the company's official, interactive process map inside Odoo. Any employee can see who does what, and in which order; clicking a step shows its work instruction, and one button takes them to the matching Odoo screen. Process managers draw, import and approve the processes.

The module does not block Odoo buttons and does not change the status of sales, purchases, stock operations or invoices. It describes, teaches and guides — it is not a workflow engine.
New in version 1.4: text formatting (size, bold, italic, underline, alignment); exact position and size; grouping and locking; tidying arrows and editing arrow labels; printing and PDF (A4/A3, splitting into pages, step list); protection when two managers edit at the same time; side panel with tabs, collapsible sections and adjustable width; hints on the form — what to click and when; choosing a small or major change at publication; “?” links to the guide; the guide in English too.
In version 1.2: connection points on every side of a shape, arrow routing based on the chosen side, arrow “jumps” at crossings, pools snapping to each other, the management dashboard (workload, handoffs between roles, portfolio, process health, reviews, read confirmations).
In version 1.1: an improved editor (colors, highlighting of shapes you can connect to, quick append/create of elements with search, alignment, minimap, grid, keyboard shortcuts), a new side panel and step-by-step process walkthrough, a comment on the responsible and automatic lane names, the process card (SIPOC + KPI), architecture levels L0–L4, tags, periodic review, read confirmation, colors carried over from draw.io/Visio.

3.Map and process walkthrough

The Processes → Process Map menu shows all published processes as cards: thumbnail, level (L0–L4), code, tags, version and owner. The cards are grouped by type.

For viewers, the process Diagram tab opens read-only. On the right is the side panel:

  • Process overview (when nothing is selected): the number of steps, roles and links, the ▷ Walk through the process button, step search and the “Who does what” list.
  • Step card (after a click): Who — the lane and its responsible, with the comment; What to do — the instruction (lists formatted); Open in Odoo — link tiles; Flow — previous and next steps. Click the next step to walk through the process to the end.
  • On the diagram, the current step is highlighted in green and the next steps with a yellow dashed line. A green 🔗 mark in a shape's corner means it has a link.

A link tile opens the matching Odoo menu, record or web page. If the link is broken or you have no access to it, the tile is red and does not work.

Search across all processes: in the search box, search by name, code, tag, diagram element (e.g. “Invoice”) or responsible (e.g. “Warehouse”). The Responsibilities menu shows which lane belongs to which department across all processes.

4.Drawing a diagram (Process Manager)

  1. All Processes → New. Fill in the name, code (e.g. SCM-07), type, level, owner and parent process (if this is a sub-process).
  2. The Diagram tab opens the editor (a green ● Editing mark in the top bar). For more room, press F or the full-screen button.
  3. Drag a shape from the palette on the left, or press N and search by name (e.g. “timer”).
  4. With a shape selected, press A — pick the next step in the dialog; it is placed and connected automatically. You can do the same from the menu next to the shape (context pad).
  5. To connect, use the context pad arrow (or C): while you draw, every shape you are allowed to connect to lights up green according to BPMN rules, and the source shape turns orange. The same happens when you drag the end of an existing arrow onto another shape.
  6. To change the text, double-click the shape (or press E), or change the name in the panel header. To change the type, use the ⇄ button in the panel or R.
  7. Changes go to the form automatically; save with Ctrl+S (Mac: ⌘+S) or the cloud icon next to the form title.

Connection points, routing and crossings

  • When you hover over a shape, connection points appear on all its sides (3 per side on a task, 4 on an event or gateway). Drag from a point to start the arrow exactly there.
  • While you draw an arrow, the points of allowed target shapes light up green; the arrow “snaps” to the nearest point (dark green point). Hold Ctrl to attach the arrow anywhere.
  • The arrow leaves from the chosen side (downwards from the bottom point, to the right from the right point) and enters the target from the chosen side; the connection point is kept when the shape is moved. Moving an arrow end to another point works the same way.
  • Where a horizontal arrow crosses a vertical one, it “jumps” over it with a small arc — it is clear that the lines do not join. This is display only: it shows in SVG/PNG export but is not written to the BPMN file.
  • When you drag a pool to the top or bottom edge of another pool, it snaps exactly to it and also aligns on the left edge.
Pool and lanes: place a “Pool/participant” from the palette; click the pool and its context pad shows “Add lane above/below” and “Divide into two/three lanes”. Each lane is usually one department or role.

The Drawing tips in the overview panel sum up these techniques; for the full list of shortcuts press ?.

5.Colors, layout, view

Colors

Select one or more shapes/arrows and, in the panel's Style section, pick a preset color (based on the GEC palette: teal, navy, orange, yellow and more) or your own Fill and Line and text. ↺ restores the default. The same palette is available from the color icon in the context pad.

Colors are stored in the standard BPMN color extension, so they show in Camunda Modeler and bpmn.io and carry over to the draw.io export.

Arranging

When several shapes are selected (Shift+click or lasso L), the panel shows Arrange: align left/center/right/top/middle/bottom and distribute evenly. While you drag a shape, orange guide lines appear and shapes snap to the grid. S (space tool) adds or removes space between shapes.

Text format: size, bold, italic, underline, alignment

Select one or more elements. In the panel's Text section:

  • A− / A+ — text size (from 9 to 32). The current size is shown in the middle; a gray number means the element has no size of its own and uses the diagram's size;
  • B / I / U — bold, italic, underline;
  • align left / center / right — for tasks, sub-processes and annotations (where the text is inside the shape);
  • Clear text format restores the default.

Shortcuts: Ctrl+B / I / U and Ctrl+Shift+> / < (size). Every change is undone in one step (Ctrl+Z), even when several elements were selected.

Diagram text size — with nothing selected, choose Small, Normal, Large or Extra large in the panel. The size applies to every element that has no size of its own — handy for showing on screen or printing.

Multi-line names — in the panel header field, Shift+Enter starts a new line and Enter finishes.

Copy style — in the “Style” section, Copy style remembers the element's colors and text format; select other elements and click Paste style.

Same size — when several tasks are selected, the Same size as the first selected button in “Arrange” gives them all the size of the first one (the center stays in place).

The text format is stored in the layout part of the BPMN file under its own namespace (odoostyle). Camunda and other tools open the file without problems; they simply do not show the format. When exporting to draw.io and importing from it, size, bold/italic/underline and alignment are kept.

Exact position and size

With one shape selected, enter X, Y, W (width) and H (height) in pixels in the panel's Position and size section and press Enter. Size can be changed for tasks, sub-processes, pools and lanes (minimum 50×40); events and gateways have the standard BPMN size.

Grouping and locking

  • Group — select several shapes → “Group and lock” → Group. Clicking any member selects the whole group, and dragging moves them all together. To select one member on its own: Alt+click. Ungroup removes the group.
  • Lock — Lock prevents the shape from being moved, resized or deleted (a padlock mark shows). Text, color, instruction and links can still be edited. Useful for pools and lanes, so they do not shift by accident.

Tidying arrows

If an arrow you moved by hand has collected many bends, select it and click Tidy the route in the panel — the arrow is rerouted along the shortest right-angled path. With several elements selected, the Tidy N arrows button straightens all selected arrows at once. Connection points (sides) you chose by hand are kept. Ctrl+Z undoes the change in one step.

Line type: solid, dashed, dotted

In BPMN, the look of a line means the type of connection, so dashes are not a free choice — you choose the connection type. Select an arrow: the panel's Line type section shows the allowed options with a picture and an explanation.

LookTypeHow you get it
Solid arrowSequence flowA normal arrow inside one pool
Slash at the startDefault flowChosen in the panel (on an arrow leaving a gateway or task)
Diamond at the startConditional flowChosen in the panel (on an arrow leaving a task); write the condition as the label
Dashed, open arrowMessage flowAutomatically, when you connect two different pools
DottedAssociation / Data associationAutomatically, when you connect an annotation, document or data store

The line color is changed in the “Style” section. Line thickness does not exist in the BPMN standard and would not carry over to other tools, so the editor does not offer it.

Arrow labels

  • Writing and editing — double-click the arrow or its label, or select the arrow and type the text in the panel header field. A single click on the label opens the arrow's panel.
  • Quick labels — on an arrow leaving a gateway, the panel has Yes / No buttons.
  • Position — a new label is placed beside the middle of the arrow and does not cover the line. You can drag the label; in the “Appearance” tab, Put the text back in place puts it beside the arrow again. With several elements selected, the same button fixes all labels at once.
  • Readability — labels of arrows, events and gateways have a thin white background, so they stay readable even when they sit on a line (also in export and print).
  • Size, bold, italic and underline also work on arrow labels (“Appearance” → “Text”).

Repeat (loop, multiple instances)

In the task and sub-process panel, Repeat: Once, Repeated (loop until a condition is met), For each, at the same time (|||) and For each, one after another (≡). The standard BPMN marker appears on the shape.

View

  • Zoom — the buttons at the bottom left, Ctrl+mouse wheel; clicking the percentage = 100%; 0 — fit to screen;
  • Side panel — the panel of a selected element has two tabs: Content (Who, instruction, links, flow, line type, repeat) and Appearance (style, text, position and size, route). The tab you opened last stays open when you move to another element. Clicking a section title collapses/expands it; dragging the panel's left edge changes its width (double-click — default). Everything is stored in the browser.
  • Minimap (M) — for finding your way in a large diagram; Grid (G); Side panel (P) — hide it if you need more room. Your choice is stored in the browser.

8.Instructions, links, responsible

Work instruction

Select a shape and fill in the Work instruction in the panel: what has to be done, which rule applies, which check is performed. A line starting with - is shown to viewers as a list; 1. as a numbered list. The text is stored in the standard BPMN documentation field, so it also shows in Camunda and Signavio.

Links

In the panel of a step without links, the Links section is open from the start; if the step already has a link, click + Add a link. At the top, choose the type with a button (chip) — each has a short hint below it explaining what it means — and type a name in the search field. Enter picks the first result.

  • Odoo screen — a menu, e.g. “Sales / Orders”. Employees see only the menus they have access to.
  • Knowledge article (Enterprise, if Knowledge is installed) — a detailed instruction. Type the title: if no such article exists, the list shows + Create “…” — the article is created and linked to the step right away; then open it from the link and fill it in.
  • Document (Enterprise, Documents) — a form, template or policy.
  • Other process — a process that continues from here, or a sub-process. Here too you can create a new process directly by typing its name.
  • Attached file — only files attached to this process (in the chatter), not those of other records.
  • Web address — an https://, http://, mailto: or internal Odoo /… link, optionally with a name shown to employees.

In the viewer, links appear on the step card in the “Open in Odoo” section, each with its type icon and label (e.g. “Knowledge article”) — one click opens it.

Only a Process Manager can create a new record from the diagram; for a Knowledge article, they also need the right to create articles in Knowledge.

Responsible (for a lane) — name and comment

Select a lane (or a pool, if it has no lanes) and, in the Responsible section, search for a department, job position (requires the Employees app), user group or user. Every step in the lane takes on this responsibility.

  • The lane name is set automatically to the responsible's name if the lane has no name or its name matched the previous responsible. Otherwise (e.g. the lane is called “Shift supervisor” and the responsible is “Warehouse”) the name is not changed and a Use “…” as lane name button appears.
  • The “Lane name follows the responsible” flag links the two: if the department is renamed in Odoo, the panel shows the new name and one click updates the lane too.
  • Comment — a clarification of the role or a note (e.g. “The check is approved by the shift supervisor”). Viewers see it on the “Who” card of every step.
Links and responsibles are also stored with a technical identifier (XML ID). So after moving to another database, standard menus still open correctly, and anything that cannot be found is marked on the diagram as broken.

9.Process card, level, tags

The Process Card tab describes the process with the SIPOC logic: Trigger, Suppliers, Inputs, Outputs, Customers, and Indicators (KPI) — what is measured, the target, the frequency and the data source. KPI targets can be changed even in a published version; the rest of the card is part of the version.

FieldMeaning
Process TypeCore (operational), supporting, management — the standard classification (Porter's value chain, the ISO 9001 process approach). There is no need to add new types; use tags for additional views.
LevelDepth in the process architecture, similar to APQC PCF: L0 value chain/category → L1 process group → L2 process → L3 sub-process → L4 activity/procedure. When you choose a parent, the level moves down by one automatically.
TagsFree, colored views (ISO 9001, Customer facing, Critical…). Managed in Configuration → Tags.

What the converter recognizes

  • All shapes of the draw.io BPMN 2.0 library, swimlanes, Cross-Functional Flowchart;
  • Shapes of the Visio BPMN Diagram template (based on shape data), Cross-Functional Flowchart lanes, Basic Flowchart;
  • Plain drawings: rectangle → task, diamond → exclusive gateway, circle/oval → event, document → data object, cylinder → data store, text → annotation;
  • Colors: shape fill and line color (default white/black colors are not carried over, so the diagram keeps the module's style).

What happens after import

  1. Lanes are compared with departments, job positions and groups with exactly the same name. Matches are linked automatically.
  2. Every lane is flagged with a ! mark: “Confirm the automatic match” or “Assign a department / role”. Shapes whose type was guessed are flagged too (“Check the element type”).
  3. A report is written to the chatter: what was converted, what was skipped and why.
Tip: in draw.io, use the More Shapes → BPMN 2.0 library and attach connectors to shapes. In Visio, use the BPMN or Cross-Functional Flowchart template. Give lanes exactly the name of the Odoo department — they are then linked automatically.

To run the lane matching again (e.g. after creating departments), click Match Lanes on the draft.

11.Review queue

The Review Queue menu collects the flagged elements of all processes: what has to be done and where. Open takes you to the process and selects that element directly.

In the diagram's top bar, the ⚠ N to review button moves through the flagged elements one by one; the overview panel lists them too. After fixing an element, click Mark as reviewed; choosing a responsible on a lane clears the flag by itself. Only Process Managers see the flags.

How do I change a process?

Only a Draft can be edited. In any other status, a hint shows at the top of the form, and the diagram's top bar has a green button that takes you straight to the editable version:

Where you areWhat you clickWhat happens
In ReviewBack to DraftThe same version becomes editable again
PublishedNew VersionA draft is created; 1.0 stays valid until you publish the new one (at publication you choose: 1.1 or 2.0)
An old version, while a new one is being preparedOpenThe version in preparation opens (no second draft is created)
DeprecatedRestore or New VersionIt is published again, or a draft is created
  • New Version creates a draft copy of the published version (including the diagram, the card and the KPIs). Whether the change is small or major is chosen at publication (see Small and major changes). When the new one is published, the old one automatically becomes “Deprecated” — no history is lost (the Versions button).
  • Deprecate deletes nothing: the process disappears from the Process Map but stays in “All Processes” (grayed out) and comes back with Restore. The button asks for confirmation.
  • By default, “All Processes” shows Current Versions — all statuses except old versions replaced by a newer one. For old ones choose the Old Versions filter, for archived ones Archived. A process is deleted permanently only with “Delete” in the Actions menu.
  • Only one version can be in preparation at a time. Publishing, deprecating and restoring are logged in the chatter.

Small and major changes (version number)

Clicking Publish opens a dialog. For the first version it only asks for confirmation. For every later version the dialog shows two cards — Small change and Major change — with the new number, examples and what happens; you publish with the button of one card (Publish as a small change or Publish as a major change), so a version cannot be published without this choice:

Small changeMajor change
New number1.0 → 1.11.x → 2.0
Exampleswording, a link, a clearer instruction, a typo, a renamed departmentnew or removed steps, another role does the work, a new control or approval, a different order
For employeesthey keep working as beforethey have to change how they work
Read confirmation (if enabled)kept: whoever confirmed the previous version does not need to confirm againasked again from everyone

Rule of thumb if in doubt: if someone who knows the old version would do something wrong with the new one, it is a major change.

In the dialog you can write What Changed — a sentence or two for the history. It shows in the chatter, in the “Version & Review” tab and in the version list (the “Kind of Change” column). You do not need to change the new version number by hand: it is set automatically at publication. Only the first version can be given a different number by hand (e.g. 3.2, if you are moving it over from a paper procedure).

Where this approach comes from: the “major.minor” number comes from software (Semantic Versioning) and is widely used in document management. ISO 9001 (7.5.3) requires change control and identifiable versions but does not prescribe a numbering scheme — here the number answers one important question: do employees need to read it again?

Two managers on one diagram

If another manager also has the draft open, the top bar shows a yellow Also editing: name mark (refreshed once a minute). Changes no longer silently overwrite each other:

  • whoever saves first has their version saved;
  • the second save is rejected and a warning appears above the diagram: who saved and when. Buttons: Download my version (a .bpmn copy) and Load the saved version. You can redo your own changes or bring in your copy (import → replace);
  • the warning appears even before you save, as soon as someone else saves — so you do not keep working in vain.

Periodic review

In the Version & Review tab, set Review Every (months) (12 by default). At publication, the Next Review date is calculated; when it is due, the process owner gets a to-do activity “Periodic process review”, and the date moves to the next cycle. The Review Due filter shows overdue processes.

Read confirmation

If Read Confirmation is enabled, employees see an I have read it button on the published process. Confirmations are stored per version: after a major change the new version has to be read again; after a small change the previous confirmations carry over (marked “Carried Over” in the list). The manager sees the Read by N button and the full list (Configuration → Read Confirmations).

Printing and PDF

In the dialog, choose:

  • Paper — A4 or A3; Orientation — automatic (based on the diagram's shape), landscape or portrait;
  • Layout — Fit on one page (the diagram fits on one sheet; if the text comes out too small, the dialog warns you) or Split into pages at 100%, 75% or 50%. When split, the sheets overlap by 6 mm and are labeled like a map (A1, B1, A2…), so they are easy to tape together;
  • Add — process details (owner, status, purpose, inputs/outputs), the step list (instruction, who performs it, links) and step numbers on the diagram that match the numbers in the list.

The preview shows how the diagram will be split. Print opens the browser's print dialog; for PDF, choose “Save as PDF” as the printer. The top of every page shows the process name, code, version and status (an unapproved version is marked in orange), the bottom shows who printed it and when. Unsaved changes are printed too. Your choices are stored in the browser.

15.FAQ

Why is my process not on the map?

The map shows only published processes. Drafts, versions in review and deprecated versions are in the All Processes menu (managers only).

A process disappeared — was it deleted?

Almost never. Deprecate and publishing a new version delete nothing. Filters in “All Processes”: Deprecated, Old Versions (replaced by a newer one), Archived. A process is deleted permanently only with “Delete” in the Actions menu.

Why can't I change the diagram?

Editing is possible only in a draft and only for Process Managers. The hint at the top of the form and the green button in the diagram's top bar (New version to edit, Back to draft to edit or Open version …) take you straight to the editable version. Details: How do I change a process?

“X saved a newer version” — what do I do?

Another manager was editing the same draft and saved first. Click Download my version, then Load the saved version, and redo your changes. Details: Two managers on one diagram.

Shortcuts don't work

Click an empty spot on the diagram — shortcuts work when the canvas is active. Ctrl+S also works in the panel fields.

Will everything come over exactly from draw.io/Visio?

A drawing made with the BPMN library — almost completely, colors included. For free-form drawings, the module guesses the shape type and flags such shapes for review. Fonts and the exact bends of lines are not carried over: connectors are redrawn at right angles.

What is not supported

  • The old Visio .vsd format (save it as .vsdx); export to Visio;
  • BPMN Choreography and Conversation diagrams;
  • Automatic execution of the process (this is not a workflow engine — on purpose);
  • Multiple languages for diagram content (names, instructions).

What is the “BPMN.io” logo in the corner?

The license of the diagram library (bpmn-js) requires this logo to stay visible. Hiding it violates the license.

16.For administrators

  • Installation / upgrade: add the bpmn_process_management folder to the addons path → Apps → Update Apps List → “BPMN Process Management” → Install (or Upgrade when updating). No additional Python packages are needed.
  • Roles: every internal user is a Viewer automatically; Process Manager is assigned on the user form.
  • Language: the administrator activates a language once (Settings → Languages → Add Language), then each user chooses it in My Preferences → Language. The whole module interface switches, including the editor and the buttons of this guide. Translation: i18n/ka.po; for a new language — i18n/bpmn_process_management.pot. The guide is available in two languages: Georgian users get the Georgian version, everyone else gets English (/bpmn_process_management/guide).
  • Scheduled action: “Business Processes: periodic review reminders” runs once a day (Settings → Technical → Scheduled Actions).
  • Multi-company: “Company” empty = the process applies to all companies; filled in = to that company only. KPIs and confirmations follow the process.
  • Link to departments: enabled if Employees (hr) is installed. Knowledge and Documents links — in Enterprise, if those apps are installed.
