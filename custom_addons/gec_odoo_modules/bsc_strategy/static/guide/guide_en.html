Balanced Scorecard - User Guide

How to set up a strategy in Odoo, fill it in every period, and use it to make decisions. Read sections 1-6 once; after that, the dashboard and this guide's question section are what you will come back to.

1. What this module does

The module turns a strategy into something that is checked every period. You write down a small number of strategic objectives, decide how each one is measured (KPIs with targets), say which objective is supposed to drive which (the strategy map), plan the work that should move them (initiatives), and then, every quarter or month, the people responsible enter the figures. The period is closed, a strategy review is held, and the decisions of that review are recorded.

It is not a KPI dashboard with extra fields. A number on its own changes nothing; the module is built around the moment where a manager looks at a red figure, asks why, and decides what to do.

The menu at a glance Dashboard - where things stand (managers) · Strategy - scorecards, strategy map, objectives, KPIs, links, initiatives · Data Entry - the figures you have to report · Reviews - strategy reviews and periods · Analysis - charts and pivot tables · Configuration - perspectives, stages, data sources, demo data · User Guide - this page.

The status colours

On Track achievement at or above the green threshold (95 % by default) · At Risk between amber (80 %) and green · Off Track below 80 % · No Data nothing reported yet, or no target to compare with.

Zero is a figure; empty is not. A KPI that has not been reported is not counted as zero. It is left out of the average and shown as "No Data". Zero complaints or zero accidents are real results and are counted.

3. Who can do what

Access levelCanTypical person
ContributorSee the strategy; enter the figures of their own KPIs; propose initiatives and edit the ones they own.Department heads, data owners
ManagerEverything above, plus design objectives, KPIs, links and initiatives; validate figures; close periods; run reviews; see the dashboard.Strategy office, management team
AdministratorEverything above, plus configure perspectives, stages and automated data sources; reopen a closed period; load and remove demo data; delete records.Odoo administrator

What the system enforces, whatever screen or tool is used:

  • A contributor enters the figure and comment of their own KPIs only. The target, the KPI and the period of a row are a manager's; a contributor cannot validate their own figure or change it once a manager has validated it.
  • A contributor proposes an initiative; only a manager can approve it, start it or close it. Who approved it, and when, is recorded automatically and cannot be typed. Once approved, its planned budget is changed by a manager only.
  • A closed period is locked for everyone except an Administrator, and only an Administrator can reopen it.
  • Figures marked as coming from Odoo data are written by the automation only - a typed figure cannot be dressed up as an automated one.
  • Connecting a KPI to Odoo data: the total is the same for everyone, but a filter ("Only count records where") may only cover records the person setting it is allowed to see.
  • Only the Demo Data window can mark data as demo, so "Remove demo data" can never delete a real scorecard.
  • Every BSC record is visible only inside its own company.

Give access in Settings → Users & Companies → Users, open the person, and choose a level in the Balanced Scorecard field. The person who installed the module is an Administrator automatically; nobody else gets access until you give it.

Someone cannot see the app? They have no Balanced Scorecard access level yet. Give them at least Contributor and ask them to reload the page.

4. Start with the demo

The fastest way to understand the module is to look at a filled-in one. Open Configuration → Demo Data and press Load Demo Data.

You get one scorecard for a full year: eight objectives in the four perspectives, eight KPIs with targets, nine cause-effect links, six initiatives, three closed quarters with figures and reviews, and the current quarter partly filled in. Texts are in the language of your interface. You are the owner of everything, so every screen shows something.

When you are done, open the same window and press Remove Demo Data. Only the demo scorecard and what is in it are deleted - your own scorecards are never touched. You can load and remove it as often as you like.

5. Setting up a scorecard

The order matters: objectives first, then the links between them, then the measures, then the work. The scorecard form shows a setup checklist that follows exactly this order and tells you, by name, what is still missing.

Step 0 - Perspectives (once, administrator)

Configuration → Perspectives. The four classic perspectives are already there. Rename them, change their order (the order is bottom-to-top on the strategy map), change the default weight, or add a fifth one. You normally do this once for the whole company.

Step 1 - Create the scorecard

  1. Strategy → Scorecards → New.
  2. Give it a name, for example Strategy 2027.
  3. Choose the Strategy Owner - the person who runs the reviews.
  4. Set Cycle Start and Cycle End (usually 1 January - 31 December) and the Review Frequency (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual or annual). Periods are generated from these when you activate.
  5. Optionally write the vision and mission on their tab, and set perspective weights on the Perspective Weights tab (leave it empty to use each perspective's default weight).

Step 2 - Objectives

On the Objectives tab type the objectives straight into the table, one line each: a short code (F1, C2, P1, L1 ...), the perspective, the name and the owner. Two to four objectives per perspective is normal; more than that makes a strategy hard to follow.

  • Weight is optional. Leave every weight at zero and all objectives of a perspective count equally.
  • Assessment Mode: most objectives are measured by KPIs. An objective that does not turn into a number (for example "Digitalise core processes") can be set to Qualitative assessment: its owner rates it green, amber or red with a short note, and it needs no KPI.

Step 3 - KPIs and targets

Open an objective (arrow at the end of its line) and fill the KPIs tab - one line per KPI.

FieldWhat to enter
NameWhat is measured, e.g. Average delivery time.
UnitFree text: %, GEL, days, points, complaints ...
DirectionHigher is better (revenue, satisfaction), Lower is better (costs, delivery time, complaints) or Inside a range (for example engagement between 70 and 85 %).
TargetThe target for each period. It is copied onto every period's row and can be changed there. For a range KPI fill Range Minimum and Range Maximum instead.
Data OwnerThe person who will be asked for the figure every period. Required.
WeightOptional, inside the objective. Leave at zero for equal weights.
Always set a target. Without one, a figure that is entered still shows "No Data", because there is nothing to compare it with. The setup checklist lists the KPIs that have no target.

The full KPI form (arrow at the end of the line) has more: a Definition tab (write down exactly how the figure is obtained - next year nobody will remember), the Baseline, the green and amber thresholds, the Target Rule (for example "last period's actual plus 5 %") and the history of all periods.

Many KPIs at once? Strategy → KPIs (or Edit KPIs in a table on the scorecard's KPIs tab) is an editable table of every KPI: choose the objective on each line and type.

Step 4 - Cause-effect links

Say which objective is supposed to drive which, and in one sentence why. Two places do the same thing:

  • on an objective, the Strategy Map tab: "This objective contributes to" and "This objective is driven by";
  • Strategy → Cause-Effect Links: the whole map as one table - faster when you write all links at once.

Each link has a hypothesis (in plain words), a strength (how strongly you believe it) and a Validated switch. Tick validated only once the figures really show the two moving together. Links are optional, but a strategy without them is just a list of goals.

Step 5 - Initiatives

On the scorecard's Initiatives tab, or in Strategy → Initiatives, add the projects and actions that should move the objectives. Details are in section 9. Initiatives are optional for activation.

Step 6 - Activate

Press Activate at the top of the scorecard. The module checks the design (every measured objective needs a KPI, every KPI a data owner) and lists all problems at once if there are any. Then it:

  • generates the periods of the cycle (for example 2027-Q1 ... 2027-Q4);
  • creates an empty row for every KPI in every period that has already started - so a cycle activated in October still collects January-September;
  • from then on reminds data owners in the last week of each period.
Next year use Copy to New Cycle on the scorecard: objectives, KPIs, weights and links are copied into a new draft; figures and periods stay with the old cycle.

6. Every period

Entering figures

Data Entry shows the figures you have to report for the open period. Click into the Actual column, type the figure, press Enter. Achievement and status are calculated immediately. Managers can open the same list for a whole scorecard with Enter Figures on the scorecard.

  • Target is filled in already; change it on the row if this period's target is different.
  • A figure that comes out Off Track needs a Variance Comment - why it happened and what is being done. The row cannot be saved without it: a red figure without an explanation cannot be acted upon.
  • Previous shows last period's actual, to catch typing mistakes.
  • Select rows and press Submit when your figures are final. A manager presses Validate to sign them off.
  • Entered a figure by mistake? Open the row and press Clear Figure - it goes back to "not reported".
Seen a figure somewhere you cannot change? Wherever a figure, KPI or objective is only shown - on a scorecard, a KPI, a review, the dashboard or the strategy map - there is a Figure, Edit or Enter / change figure button next to it that opens the place where it can be changed.

Closing a period

Reviews → Periods, open the period and press Close Period.

  • If figures are missing, closing is refused and the missing KPIs are listed. Close Anyway closes regardless - use it consciously.
  • A closed period is locked: nobody can change its figures. Only an administrator can Reopen it, and the reopening is written to the scorecard's history.
  • Closing prepares the next period and opens the period's strategy review.

The strategy review

When a period closes, its review is created as a draft: the score and completion of that period are frozen, and the off-track objectives are listed. The strategy owner gets a to-do. The meeting is yours:

  1. Open the review (Reviews → Strategy Reviews, or Start/Open the review on the dashboard). Add the participants.
  2. Go through Figures of the Period and the Off-track Objectives. Write what the figures say on the Findings tab.
  3. For each off-track objective decide something. Act on it creates a corrective initiative linked to the objective and to this review.
  4. Write the Decisions - what, who, by when. A review cannot be closed without decisions: a review that decides nothing is a report.
  5. If the review changed objectives, targets or links, say so on Strategy Adjustment. Press Close Review. Board Pack prints the PDF for the board for this period.

7. The dashboard

Dashboard (managers) answers in one screen: where does the strategy stand, what moved, and what does somebody have to act on? Choose the scorecard and the period at the top - you can look at any past period, not only the current one.

8. The strategy map

Strategy → Strategy Map draws the scorecard: perspectives as horizontal bands, objectives as cards coloured by status, links as arrows from cause up to effect. A dashed arrow is a hypothesis nobody has checked against data; a solid one is validated. Thicker means more strongly believed.

  • Click a card: its KPIs, figures, comments, links and initiatives appear on the right. Click again to close.
  • Click an arrow: the hypothesis, with a button to mark it validated.
  • Highlight proven links fades every unchecked assumption.
  • How to read it: find the red card near the top and follow the arrows down - those are the causes your own strategy nominated for that result.

9. Initiatives in detail

  • Every initiative serves at least one objective of the same scorecard.
  • Stages: Proposed → Under Assessment → Approved → In Progress → Done (or Cancelled). Drag the card or click the stage on the form.
  • Approval needs a planned budget - an initiative without resources is a wish, not a plan. Done needs a result on the Result tab.
  • On the Execution tab an approved initiative can create a Project (for big work) or a single Task. Progress then follows the tasks the moment one changes: a task counts as done when it is marked Done or dragged into the Done column; a cancelled task is left out; a task with subtasks counts through its subtasks. Each change of progress is recorded in the initiative's chatter. Untick Track Progress from Project to type the progress yourself.
  • The project shows an Initiatives button that leads back to the initiative.
  • Overdue (end date passed, still running) and over-budget initiatives appear on the dashboard.

10. Figures filled from Odoo (optional)

Some figures already exist in Odoo - revenue, orders, won deals, deliveries, completed tasks. Such a KPI does not have to be typed in.

  1. Open the KPI. Under the targets a grey box says the owner types the figure in. Press Connect to Odoo data.
  2. Pick a ready-made measure, for example Accounting: Invoiced revenue or CRM: Win rate (%). Only the apps installed in your database are listed. Each one explains what it counts.
  3. Optional: Only count records where narrows it - one sales team, one project, one product category.
  4. Look at What it would report now: the figure for the current period and how many records it comes from. Then press Connect.

The KPI box turns green and shows where the figure comes from. Every open period is filled at once and refreshed every night.

  • Money is always in the company currency, without tax, and revenue is net of credit notes.
  • A query that finds no records leaves the figure empty rather than publishing a zero.
  • A figure that moves more than the KPI's deviation limit from the previous period is held until the owner confirms or rejects it.
  • A figure somebody typed before the KPI was connected is kept. Typing over an automated figure keeps yours too. Clear Figure hands the row back to Odoo.
  • Type In Instead disconnects the KPI; figures already pulled stay.

A figure the list does not have can be built by an administrator in Configuration → KPI Data Sources (which records, which date decides the period, count / sum / share). It then appears in the same window under A data source defined by your administrator.

Automate only unambiguous figures. Satisfaction, quality and capability stay manual - and that is correct.

11. How scores are calculated

DirectionAchievementExample
Higher is betteractual ÷ target × 100NPS 35 against 40 → 87.5 % At Risk
Lower is bettertarget ÷ actual × 100 (actual 0 → 100 %)Delivery 4.2 days against 3 → 71.4 % Off Track
Inside a range100 % inside the range; proportional outside itEngagement 66 % with range 70-85 → 94.3 % At Risk
  • Achievement is capped at 150 %, so one very good figure cannot hide two bad ones.
  • Status: green from 95 %, amber from 80 % (each KPI can have its own thresholds).
  • The objective score is the weighted average of its KPIs; the perspective score the weighted average of its objectives; the scorecard index the weighted average of the perspectives. All weights zero → equal weights.
  • KPIs without a figure are left out of the average entirely - they never count as zero.
  • A qualitative objective scores 100 (green), 85 (amber) or 50 (red).

12. Questions and problems

I entered a figure but the status says "No Data".

The row has no target (target 0 on a "higher is better" KPI). Enter the target on the row, and set it on the KPI so future periods get it automatically.

The figure is exactly 0. How do I report it?

Typing 0 over the empty 0 changes nothing, so there is nothing to save. Switch on Reported on the row instead - the 0 is then a reported figure and counts.

I cannot save a figure.

The figure is off track and the Variance Comment is empty. Write why, then save.

I cannot change a figure any more.

The period is closed. Ask an administrator to reopen it (Reviews → Periods → Reopen).

Data Entry is empty.

Either no scorecard is active, or no KPI has you as its data owner, or there is no open period. Remove the filters in the search bar to see everybody's figures.

The scorecard cannot be activated.

The message lists every problem. Usually an objective has no KPI - add one, or set the objective to qualitative assessment.

Why is a past quarter not shown in Data Entry?

Data Entry shows open periods only. If an old period is still open, it is listed too; once closed, its figures are visible in Analysis, on the period and in the review.

How do I look at an earlier quarter?

On the dashboard choose the period at the top. The board pack printed from a review is always for that review's period.

Can I change the weights or thresholds later?

Yes. Scores are calculated on reading, so a change is visible at once - also for past periods. Reviews keep the score they had when they were prepared.

TermMeaning

BaselineThe value before the strategy started; reference point and basis for "baseline plus %" targets.
Board PackThe PDF summary of a scorecard for one period.
CompletionThe share of a period's figures that have been reported.
Data ownerThe person who reports a KPI's figure every period.
HypothesisThe assumption behind a cause-effect link, in plain words.
Strategy ownerThe person responsible for the scorecard and its reviews.
Target ruleHow the next period's target is proposed: same as last period, last actual plus %, baseline plus %, or empty.
